# Why "savings per engaged member" needs a comparison group
*One-page memo · Faridoon Farahi*

**The issue.** Programs that target high-cost members and report pre/post savings on those who engaged will show large savings even if the program does nothing. Two forces drive this:

1. **Regression to the mean.** Members are chosen because last year was expensive. Part of an expensive year is bad luck — a hospitalization, a fracture — and bad luck does not repeat. Their cost falls next year on its own. In a simulated Medicare-like population, selecting the top 10% by prior cost and doing nothing produces a **35% cost drop** the following year.
2. **Engagement selection.** Members who answer the phone and complete a medication review differ from those who don't. Comparing engaged to non-engaged members mixes the program's effect with those differences.

**Why it matters now.** Under fee-for-service vendor contracts, an optimistic savings number is a marketing problem. Under value-based or shared-savings contracts, it is a financial one: the client's actuary will reconstruct the number, and the gap between "reported" and "defensible" becomes a credibility gap at renewal.

**The fix.** Compare engaged members to a matched group of members who looked the same *before* the program — same prior cost, same risk, same chronic burden — and measure the difference in how the two groups changed. In the same simulation, this **matched difference-in-differences** finds ~0% when the program has no effect, and ~−10% when the true effect is −10%. The dollar figure is smaller than the naive one, but it is the one a finance team will sign off on.

**What the client should be shown.**
- The matched comparison group and how it was built
- Balance diagnostics (the two groups look alike on paper before the program)
- The effect with a confidence interval, on cost and on hospitalizations / ED visits
- A placebo test on a year with no program
- An intention-to-treat number on all targeted members — that is what a plan actually pays for

**Bottom line.** Reporting the conservative, matched estimate first is not leaving money on the table. It is the only number that survives the room where the contract gets renewed.
