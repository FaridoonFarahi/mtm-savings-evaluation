# Why "savings per engaged member" needs a comparison group
*One-page memo · Faridoon Farahi*

Programs that target high-cost members and report pre/post savings on those who engaged will show large savings even if the program does nothing. Two things drive this.

1. Regression to the mean. Members are chosen because last year was expensive. Part of an expensive year is bad luck (a hospitalization, a fracture), and bad luck does not repeat, so their cost falls the next year on its own. In a simulated Medicare-like population, selecting the top 10% by prior cost and doing nothing produces a 35% cost drop the following year.
2. Engagement selection. Members who answer the phone and complete a medication review differ from those who don't. Comparing engaged to non-engaged members mixes the program's effect with those differences.

## Why it matters now

Under fee-for-service vendor contracts, an optimistic savings number is a marketing problem. Under value-based or shared-savings contracts it is a financial one. The client's actuary will reconstruct the number, and if the reported figure is far from what holds up, the vendor loses credibility at renewal.

## The fix

Compare engaged members to a matched group of members who looked the same *before* the program (same prior cost, same risk, same chronic burden) and measure the difference in how the two groups changed. In the same simulation, this matched difference-in-differences finds ~0% when the program has no effect, and ~−10% when the true effect is −10%. The dollar figure is smaller than the naive one, but a finance team will sign off on it.

## What the client should be shown

- The matched comparison group and how it was built
- Balance diagnostics showing the two groups looked alike before the program
- The effect with a confidence interval, on cost and on hospitalizations / ED visits
- A placebo test on a year with no program
- An intention-to-treat number on all targeted members, since that is what a plan actually pays for

## Bottom line

Report the conservative, matched estimate first. It is the only number that holds up when the contract comes up for renewal.
